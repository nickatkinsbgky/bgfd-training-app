Training expectations and standards

These times support the standard times used in Personnel Performance. They are not one combined stopwatch. NFPA 1710 times the system, from the tone to arrival. NIST Technical Note 1661 timed crews on a 2,000 ft², two-story house and published gaps, not pass/fail skill sheets. UL FSRI and department skill sheets timed the task itself.

A one-story ranch will run faster than the NIST house. Time drills in full PPE, on air, with the tools the company carries. Rows marked training target are built from the measured gaps so a company has a clock. They are not a published NIST pass/fail.

Inward residential door, open and controlled30 seconds proficient, 60 seconds to qualifyTraining target. NIST held this at 10 seconds so it would not skew other tasks. That 10 seconds is not a skill standard.
Break a window for vent-enter-search10 to 15 secondsUL FSRI technical-panel average. Experiments used 10 seconds.

Ladders and ventilation25–30% faster than a short crewNIST. Four ladders were raised every evolution.
Whole first-due task set5.1 minutes faster than three people, 7 minutes faster than twoNIST, same 22 tasks on that house.

First engine travel4 minutes, 90% of firesNFPA 1710

Full first alarm, low or medium hazard8 minutes travel, 90%NFPA 1710
Fifteen firefighters assembled, from first engine at the hydrant3 minutes faster with five-person companies than four-personNIST. Two- and three-person companies never reached 15 in the experiments.
Backup line to the door or stairs16% faster start, 9% faster finish, with a short staggerNIST

A medium-growth fire hits 1 megawatt in 5 minutes and 2 megawatts in just over 7. A fast fire hits those marks in 2.5 and about 3.5 minutes. NIST found those fires were likely already beyond the room of origin before arrival, for every crew size.
